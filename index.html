Why Easter Moves Every Year

Christmas is always December 25. Easter, meanwhile, wanders across more than a month of the calendar — as early as March 22 and as late as April 25 in the Western church. The reason is that Easter is a lunisolar feast: its date is set by both the Sun and the Moon. The rule, fixed at the First Council of Nicaea in 325 AD, says Easter Sunday is the first Sunday after the first full moon on or after March 21, the ecclesiastical spring equinox. Because the lunar cycle and the seven-day week slide against each other every year, the resulting Sunday lands on a different date each time. The calculator above runs the actual computus — the medieval algorithm for this date — for any year you type.

That full moon is not the one you see in the sky, by the way. The Church uses an ecclesiastical moon, a tidy mathematical approximation of the lunar cycle (the 19-year Metonic cycle), so the "full moon" in the rule can differ from the astronomical one by a day or two. The approximation keeps the arithmetic doable with pen and paper — which mattered enormously when monks were computing Easter tables by hand for centuries in advance.

The Computus: A 1,200-Year-Old Algorithm

The procedure this tool runs is essentially the one published by the English monk Dionysius Exiguus in 525 AD and popularized by the Venerable Bede. Given the year, it grinds through modular arithmetic — remainders after division by 19 (the golden number, locating the year in the Metonic cycle), by 4, 7, 100 and 400 — and out pops the month and day. It looks like numerology, but every step corrects for a real astronomical cycle: the 19-year return of moon phases to the same calendar dates, the 28-year return of weekday patterns, the century corrections of the Gregorian calendar.

Carl Friedrich Gauss, the great German mathematician, published a celebrated simplification in 1800 that computes Western Easter in a handful of steps. The version here is the full Gregorian form, valid for every year from 1583 onward — and, by extension, as far into the future as you care to type. Try year 3000: the arithmetic does not care.

Western vs. Orthodox Easter

Flip the toggle above and the same year often gives a different Sunday. Two things separate the traditions. First, most Orthodox churches still compute Easter from the Julian calendar, which currently runs 13 days behind the Gregorian calendar — so their March 21 (the equinox anchor) is our April 3. Second, Orthodox Easter must fall after Jewish Passover, an extra constraint the Western calculation dropped. The tool handles the first difference by running the Julian computus and then converting the result to the Gregorian dates on your wall calendar.

The two Easters sometimes coincide — they did in 2025, and will again in 2028 — and sometimes land weeks apart, as in 2024 (Western March 31, Orthodox May 5). There have been serious ecumenical proposals to fix a common date, most recently discussions around a shared celebration, but the calendars remain distinct. For planners, the practical point is simple: always check which Easter your guests, staff, or customers observe.

The Holy Days Around Easter

Easter is the peak of a whole season, and the tool lists the key dates so you can plan around them. Working backward: Good Friday (two days before) and Palm Sunday (seven days before) open Holy Week; Ash Wednesday, 46 days before Easter Sunday, begins Lent — the 40 fasting days plus six Sundays. Working forward: Easter Monday is a public holiday across much of Europe, and Pentecost, 49 days after Easter, closes the Easter season. Because every one of these is defined relative to Easter, they all inherit its wanderlust — which is why school spring breaks and even some tax deadlines shift from year to year.

Two movable feasts catch travelers most often. Carnival / Mardi Gras is the day before Ash Wednesday — the famous blowout before the Lenten fast — so its date swings with Easter too. And in the Orthodox tradition, the entire Holy Week shifts together, meaning Athens, Moscow, and Addis Ababa may be celebrating while Rome and New York are back at work.

The Earliest and Latest Possible Easters

In the Western calendar, Easter can fall on March 22 at the earliest (last in 1818, next in 2285 — you will wait a while) and April 25 at the latest (last in 1943, next in 2038). The full 35-day spread means "spring" means very different things depending on the year: an April 25 Easter can be warm enough for outdoor services in northern climates, while a March 22 Easter can still be buried in snow. Orthodox Easter's window is shifted later by the calendar difference, running from April 4 to May 8 in Gregorian terms.

Statisticians have worked out the frequencies: in the long run, April 19 is the most common Western Easter date, and March 22 the rarest. The distribution is not even because the lunar and weekly cycles interact unevenly — another small delight for anyone who enjoys calendar trivia.

Easter Eggs, Bunnies, and Older Springs

Why eggs and rabbits? Both are ancient symbols of new life that spring festivals used long before Christianity. Eggs were forbidden during the Lenten fast in medieval Europe, so the eggs laid during those 40 days were hard-boiled, decorated, and eaten in a feast on Easter morning — the original Easter eggs. The Easter bunny arrives via German immigrants to America in the 1700s, from the "Osterhase," a hare that supposedly laid colored eggs for good children. Hares, famously fertile and active at the spring equinox, had symbolized renewal in European folklore for centuries.

The English word "Easter" itself probably comes from Eostre, a Germanic spring goddess mentioned by Bede — one of the few pagan names Christianity absorbed rather than replaced. Most other languages kept the Jewish root: Pâques (French), Pascua (Spanish), Pasqua (Italian) all descend from Pesach, Passover, a reminder that the Last Supper was a Passover meal.

Planning Around a Moving Feast

A movable Easter has real logistical consequences. School districts set spring break around it; airlines and hotels price the weeks differently; retailers plan the second-biggest candy season of the year (Americans buy over 16 billion jelly beans each Easter) against a date that can shift by five weeks. HR departments field time-off requests for Good Friday and Easter Monday, which are public holidays in many countries but ordinary workdays in the United States — where only about a dozen states observe Good Friday publicly.

The practical move is to look up the next few years at once. The calculator gives you any single year instantly; jot down the coming five and you can spot collisions with weddings, conferences, and travel early. Note the 2027 date especially — Western Easter falls on March 28, unusually early, which compresses the post-Christmas planning window for spring events.

Common Questions About the Date

Is Easter ever in May? Not in the Western church — April 25 is the limit. Orthodox Easter can reach May 8 in Gregorian terms (as in 2024's May 5). Why did Easter and Passover separate? The Council of Nicaea wanted Easter on a Sunday and independent of the Jewish calendar's announcements, so the two feasts that coincided in the Gospels now often diverge. Could the date be fixed? Proposals surface regularly — the World Council of Churches has discussed it — but no consensus exists, and the movable feast is now woven into liturgy, law, and commerce deeply enough that fixing it would be its own upheaval.

Does the Southern Hemisphere care? Easter is a northern-spring festival by origin, so in Australia and South America it lands in autumn — chocolate eggs and cooling weather, a combination locals defend fiercely. The date math is identical worldwide; only the season flips.

Frequently asked questions

When is Easter 2027?

Western Easter Sunday in 2027 falls on March 28, 2027 — unusually early. Orthodox Easter in 2027 falls on May 2, 2027. Type any year into the calculator above for both traditions plus the surrounding holy days.

Why does Easter change date every year?

Easter is set by both the Sun and the Moon: it is the first Sunday after the first ecclesiastical full moon on or after March 21. Because the lunar cycle and the week slide against each other, the resulting Sunday lands on a different date each year, between March 22 and April 25.

What is the difference between Western and Orthodox Easter?

Most Orthodox churches compute Easter on the Julian calendar (13 days behind the Gregorian) and require it to fall after Passover. The two Easters sometimes coincide and sometimes fall weeks apart — in 2024 they were March 31 (Western) and May 5 (Orthodox).

What are the earliest and latest possible Easter dates?

In the Western church, Easter can fall as early as March 22 (next in 2285) and as late as April 25 (next in 2038). April 19 is the most common date over the long run.

How is the date of Easter calculated?

By the computus, an algorithm dating to the 6th century: modular arithmetic on the year locates it in the 19-year lunar (Metonic) cycle and applies Gregorian century corrections. This calculator runs that algorithm live for any year and either tradition.

What holy days surround Easter?

Ash Wednesday (46 days before) starts Lent, Palm Sunday (7 days before) and Good Friday (2 days before) open Holy Week, Easter Monday follows, and Pentecost (49 days after) closes the season. All move with Easter, which is why spring breaks shift yearly.
